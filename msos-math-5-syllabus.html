📋 Math Course Overview

This course focuses on visual instruction to build deep conceptual understanding. The math curriculum covers 16 topics including place value, decimal operations, multiplication and division of fractions, volume, and geometry. Lessons start with student-led problem-solving before introducing formal math rules.

Through partner Number Talks and collaborative problem solving, students will learn to value different mathematical strategies, listen to peers’ reasoning, and work together to find solutions.

Students will complete school-wide STAR Standardized Testing at the beginning, middle, and end of the school year to monitor their overall academic progress.

📚 Course Outline by Quarter — Math 5 (enVisionMATH 2.0)

Q1 — Place Value & Whole-Number / Decimal Operations

  • Topic 1: Understand Place Value (through thousandths)
  • Topic 2: Add and Subtract Decimals to Hundredths
  • Topic 3: Fluently Multiply Multi-Digit Whole Numbers
  • Topic 4: Use Models and Strategies to Multiply Decimals

Q2 — Division & Fractions with Common Denominators

  • Topic 5: Use Models and Strategies to Divide Whole Numbers
  • Topic 6: Use Models and Strategies to Divide Decimals
  • Topic 7: Use Equivalent Fractions to Add and Subtract Fractions

Q3 — Fractions, Data & Volume

  • Topic 8: Apply Understanding of Multiplication to Multiply Fractions
  • Topic 9: Apply Understanding of Division to Divide Fractions
  • Topic 10: Represent and Interpret Data (line plots, bar/line graphs)
  • Topic 11: Understand Volume Concepts (rectangular prisms)

Q4 — Measurement, Expressions, Coordinate Plane & Geometry

  • Topic 12: Convert Measurements (customary and metric)
  • Topic 13: Write and Interpret Numerical Expressions
  • Topic 14: Graph Points on the Coordinate Plane
  • Topic 15: Algebra — Analyze Patterns and Relationships
  • Topic 16: Geometric Measurement — Classify Two-Dimensional Figures

📝 Homework Policy

As per school policy, students are expected to allocate time for a maximum of 50 minutes of homework per night. Your child’s 5th grade teachers work together to ensure that students receive a balanced homework regime aimed at creating a positive learning environment that nurtures students’ intellectual growth.

Students are expected to record assignments daily in their school planner. Homework and important reminders are also posted on Google Classroom for students and families. Parents are expected to check the student’s planner daily and support their child in time management and follow-through.

Late work will be accepted; however, submitting assignments on time is an important part of developing responsibility. Late submissions may negatively affect a student’s participation grade.

🤝 Classroom Expectations

🎓 Academic Integrity

Honesty and integrity are essential values in our classroom.

Teachers have the responsibility to:

  • Structure conditions during testing to alleviate the possibility of cheating
  • Specify the types of collaboration that are discouraged and those that are encouraged
  • Teach students summarization of material and the distinction between summarizing and copying

Students have the responsibility to:

  • Avoid situations that might contribute to cheating or plagiarizing
  • Avoid unauthorized assistance
  • Use sources in the prescribed manner outlined by the teacher

🕗 Absences & Tardiness

Students may remain at home in the case of illness, accident, quarantine, or a death in the family.

The teacher will help the student make up missing assignments after a short absence. For prolonged absences, families must notify the teacher so a plan can be developed for completing missing work. Repeated or prolonged absences may work against the student’s academic progress and possibly the student’s right to continue in attendance.

Students are marked tardy after the 7:45 a.m. bell. Explanatory notes from parents and conferences may be advisable.
